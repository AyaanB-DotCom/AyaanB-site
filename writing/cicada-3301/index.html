Cicada 3301 Research Paper

Research on the Cicada 3301 puzzles, the community that formed around them, Liber Primus, and the distinction from a later ransomware group using the same name.

Course paper · approximately 20 minutes

Group assignment. This paper was written as a collaborative course project. Contributor names are omitted from this public archive for privacy.

01

Origins

For most people, January 4th, 2012 was just an average day. For others, it was the beginning of one of the greatest mysteries in the cybersecurity world. A social media known as 4chan was highly popular and influential in 2012. It was simply an imageboard website, similar to what popular platforms such as Reddit are today. 4chan was a place where anyone could discuss anything. However, there was something extremely peculiar posted on January 4th. There was a message signed by 3301 that simply said: “We are looking for highly intelligent individuals. To find them, we have devised a test...” This was the first puzzle among others that was created by this mysterious group.

To solve the first puzzle, users had to use steganography to find hidden text within the image. However, the puzzle did not stop here. Once the secret text was found, the text led to a book cipher and other forum posts. This was not the work of just some amateurs trying to confuse people on the internet. What remains striking is that we still don’t know who this was, or why.

The mystery of Cicada 3301 was different than anything that had been seen before. The challenges required that users go to physical locations across California, Australia, South Korea, and Poland. When people would go to these locations, they were presented with a QR code attached to the logo of Cicada 3301. This meant that players around the globe had to work together to progress the challenges due to the fact that the challenge has bridged the physical and virtual landscape. However, this also meant that Cicada 3301 was a highly technical group located across the world. They were either very vast and coordinated or had financial means to travel.

02

The First Puzzle

To give an example of what the very first puzzle posted consisted of, there’s an article posted on Boxentriq that explains how to complete it step by step. Step one involved using that message on 4chan and downloading it as an image. Once downloaded, a binary analysis was done to reveal what’s inside the image. The results show TIBERIVS CLAVDIVS CAESAR says, “cipher text”. The word Caesar indicates that the cipher text needs to be decrypted using Caesar cipher. The cipher text lxxt>33m2mkyv2gsq3q=w]02ntk is then put into a Caesar cipher decoder. The shift number was guessed and ended up being 4, giving a link to the users. There was an image of a duck and was used to represent a fake end saying, “Whoops just decoys this way”. It also importantly said “Looks like you can’t guess how to get the message out”. This second message hints to the user to use the tool Outguess. This tool is a more advanced steganography tool that is free and can not only hide messages within an image but can also extract messages within one. So, when that image link is pasted a book code is given along with a reddit link.

Step two involves using the images of a welcome mat and multiple paintings of a character found on the reddit link in the Outguess. The results just ensure the user taking the test that they are on the right track for completing the test as the messages are signed by 3301. On the reddit page the header which looked like an old writing style ended up being a Mayan numbering system. This String was “A2E7J6IC78H0J” posted next to it. They were then able to see that the numbers matched the Mayan numbering system therefore they began mapping the letters to the Mayan numbering system as well. The numbers are 10, 2, 14, 7, 19, 6, 18, 12, 7, 8, 17, 0, 19, 7, 14, 18, 14, 19, 13, 0, 1, 2, 0. From the subreddit they used these numbers to subtract from the letters to create a new letter which ended up being a message about King Arthur. It then uses the book code to make that message even smaller to a message stating “Call us at us telephone number two one four three nine oh nine six oh eight”. The actual phone number does not work so if you wanted to complete the puzzle you would just have to read the transcript which just asks for you to find the other prime numbers in the original image. Once you find them multiply it together to the other prime number given 3301 and use the result and add .com to the end of it to get access to more information. Once brought to the site, there is no more information, as the developers did not think people would make it this far. They then updated the site with the picture of a cicada showing coordinates of 14 locations and only a limited amount of people were allowed to register for email and from here the public communication is cut off. The reason why is once an email is registered the communication between cicada and the test user becomes private. This means that the first test most likely comes to an end once you reach this stage.

03

The Cicada Community

From these puzzles came the cicada community solvers. This was a community that sprung out to spread knowledge and pieces of information that can lead to the next step of the puzzle being solved. The progress of how to solve the puzzle is left transparent to all with no steps left unhidden. How did this community begin? As mentioned before, it originated on 4chan but quickly became hosted on an IRC (internet relay chat) channel. This became the natural hub of solving attempts gaining the reputation for solving where all information was shared. IRC was different from 4chan since its a chat with anonymous people without an identity tied to them. IRC instead allows for nicknames to be associated with other people you are chatting the puzzle to about. But because IRC still had this consistent problem of little to no moderation, the community solvers moved to discord. It was a free and scalable platform gaining popularity with a set of convenient tools that can be used that the other platforms lacked such as being able to implement guidelines to help prevent trolls or people that are not supposed to be within the chat. Tools that are shared involve CyberChef (open source app allowing for encode/decode), 3301 hash alarm (hashes website you come across in hopes to find the page that the book Liber Primus asks you to look for.) In a DEFCON conference one of the speakers in the video (DEFCON 31 STORIES –CRACKING CICADA) states how what differentiates this community from other solving communities is “no PGP = no Cicada”. This means any clue that the community found that is not cryptographically signed is fake and the community would make a rule on discord to not accept it. The community also set in rules to help make this chat a workshop of study sessions where people would come together and give feedback/teach on what they found instead of critiquing/ridiculing someone. Now discord has a server with over 10,000 members in it! This can be a useful group to work with if still active, in case Cicada decides to start publishing more puzzles.

04

Name and Symbolism

Based on the prime numbers you had to add, it was still a mystery as to why they were called Cicada 3301. According to the National Museum of Natural History Smithsonian site, cicadas live 13- or 17-year cycles. These numbers are both prime just like how 3301 is prime. There was no real evidence as to why it is called cicada, but cicadas shed their skin to become adults. Therefore, this is an analogy to how the users should be when working on puzzles. They should be putting all their other tasks behind when it comes to being able to focus truly on completing the puzzle which is used as a comparison to being shedding skin. That analogy made sense to include here despite not being verified by any other party. To further dive into their origins, where does their logo come from? The logo is similar to the movie, Silence of the Lambs from 1991. They are both similar as the images have secret meanings. In silence of the lambs its alternative depiction can be seen as a skull made of naked women.

05

Perspectives on Who Was Behind Cicada

According to Joel Erikson (on the podcast he had with Nostalgia Nerd, a youtuber who talks about games, software, hardware, programs) who was able to solve some of Cicada 3301’s challenges, he explains who he believes was not behind this. Beforehand it’s important to note that Eriksson was not someone who had a lack of intelligence on the subject, as he was from Sweden and was a vulnerability researcher/exploit developer who has spoken at events like Defcon. This is important to note as it was almost unlikely to complete this puzzle if you had no deep experience/interest in the field. He describes that the people who were not behind this were groups such as the CIA or NSA because then they would be hypocrites. One reason is that one of the goals of the government is to advocate for freedom of speech, but when it came to talking about Cicada, they could not talk about it to anyone. In addition, if it was the government they would then be spying on their own people which is against amendments such as the 4th being that it protects people from unreasonable search and seizure. Therefore, he does not think the government would try to be sneaking around on 4chan as there are too many trolls on there, he states. For an official government to be on a place with these trolls around pretending to be Cicada and posting fake clues and hints would not be very government like. Eriksson expresses his disappointment that he was not recruited despite completing the puzzle. He was disappointed because many of the people that completed the puzzle did it in groups of 20-50 people and so when it came time to register for an email to get on the private server everybody bandwagoned on to join from that group which took away many slots for those who individually completed it to get a chance to participate in the secret tasks Cicada 3301 had. Eriksson thinks that many in the group did not share the work towards completing the challenge equally resulting in “dummies” to enter this area that highly intelligent people enter. Because of this Cicada put out a post saying “We want the best, not the followers” in the message from Cicada describing how unfortunately your email did not get to go through the first come, first served stage. This outcome was especially difficult for Eriksson to accept, as he did end up completing it himself. With all that said other conspiracy theories such as Cicada being run by a cult or a terrorist group remain speculative and cannot be proven as no religious ideologies/violent evidence can be found in any clues/info accessible to the public. Therefore, instead Eriksson sees a smaller private group of old school hackers behind Cicada.

06

Motives and Dead Man’s Switch

There were many speculations as to why the group wanted these people to solve their puzzles. Based on leaked communications that were found, Cicada 3301 was believed to have been found by some friends that believed that censorship is wrong, and that privacy is a right. Many believed that Cicada 3301 consisted of members as part of the Cypherpunk movement in the 1980’s and 1990’s, which was a group of activists who advocated for the use of privacy-enhancing technologies as well as strong cryptography. The group’s primary goal was to develop privacy focused software, which would be revealed through their challenges.

One of the first software development projects was assigned to successful candidates who were able to solve the first puzzle. The people who were able to solve the first puzzle were invited to a private chat room over the TOR network (dark web) in which they were told about why they were recruited. On the TOR network, the type of extension seen is .Onion. This type of routing makes the data source and destination untraceable by having the data get encrypted multiple times just like the layers of an onion. The special browser to access these extensions is the Tor Browser. The dark web involves the buying/selling of guns, drugs, etc. Before Tor was made public it was used by the navy to secure private communications. It was eventually made public for privacy advocates. People that made it this far had mentioned the software they wanted to create was called “Dead Man’s Switch” and Cicada 3301 wanted the people who solved the puzzle to work together on this software. This software was supposed to serve as a fail-safe mechanism which would release all information from any individual who would be considered a “whistle-blower”. However, their plan soon fails to exist due to the dynamics of the people who solved the puzzle. Although the group had successfully recruited “the best”, this also meant that they recruited highly independent people who struggled with working collaboratively. To further add on, there was a clear lack of guidance from Cicada 3301 on how they wanted the project to be structured. This caused many collisions because each person had their own idea on how they wanted the software to look like. Due to these reasons, the developers started to lose interest, and the project started to dwindle. While the successors of the first puzzle were working on Dead Man’s Switch, there were two significant events that happened. Cicada 3301 released a second puzzle, and the group was receiving the effects of the Edward Snowden leaks.

07

Disappearance After Snowden

Around May of 2013, Cicada 3301 ceased all production of the software and seemingly disappeared from the internet for no reason why. The dark web forum where Cicada operated was taken down without any notice, and the winners of the second puzzle were not given any information either. Many speculate that this was due to the Edward Snowden leaks, as the timing seems to align perfectly. The Snowden leaks were very significant because they made the entire cryptography community very paranoid and had everyone searched for signs of NSA surveillance, as well as any backdoors. Due to the already present conflicts of the Dead Man’s switch and the new paranoia risen from the Snowden leaks, Cicada 3301 ceased to exist for a short period of time.

08

Liber Primus

In 2014, Cicada 3301 emerged once again from its hiatus to release a third and final puzzle. This puzzle was not like its predecessors and was far more complex than anything anyone had ever encountered. The puzzle started off with an image which led to a message which led to a book. This book led to a link which led to what was known as the “Liber Primus”. The Liber Primus was a digital book which translates to “First Book” in Latin. The book was written in a runic alphabet which was introduced by Cicada in their earlier puzzles. The translation was deemed nearly impossible due to its heavy layers of encryption. There are many different reports which are inconsistent with how many pages are translated since the challenge was released, but they all vary around 19 pages. The first fifteen pages were cracked and essentially acted as a tutorial for the rest of the book. However, the rest of the book consisted of encryption algorithms that were completely different than just a simple shift cipher. The text is encrypted using a polyalphabetic cipher which requires a huge and complex key. To further elaborate, the text is encrypted using an OTP, which means that it’s unbreakable without the exact key. This means that not even a quantum computer would be able to solve this encryption. In the past decade, no one has been able to solve this mysterious book, and its secrets remain unrevealed. Due to the fact that Cicada 3301 relied on TOR sites and obscure subreddits and Dropbox accounts, it’s very much possible that the clues are on sites that are no longer online. Many people claim that the book is impossible to solve, and that Cicada 3301 was simply trying to bring forth a group of highly skilled and technical people together to face the problem of digital privacy and security.

09

Later Silence and a Namesake Ransomware Group

The last message that was ever produced by Cicada 3301 was released in 2017 which stated, “Beware of false paths”. Always verify PGP signature from 7A35090F. 3301”. By then, Cicada 3301 had become an internet phenomenon. Many suggested that it was just a simple ARG game, and others tried putting fake websites and information to gain attention. Since then, there have been no communications or messages from Cicada 3301. There has also been no progress in the deciphering of Liber Primus. No next step, no new clues, no guidance.

It’s important that we distinguish this puzzle from the recent development in 2024 of a group also called Cicada 3301 but as a group that’s performing ransomware as a service attack. Ransomware essentially is an attack where someone breaks into your system, encrypts private files, and demands a ransom payment for their safe release. RAAS is using that ransomware to then be sent to other criminals who can alter/use it. In this case, a group known as Cicada 3301 was found sending stolen data to other criminals. The ransomware attacks operating systems like Windows and Linux. This malware (ransomware) was created using a programming language called rust (released in 2006). It was built here as it has high performance/memory, which is important in reducing bugs, so encryption of the victims’ files is done quickly. The data that was sent to other criminals was put on the dark web, which we mentioned before. Data that’s released to the dark web is a vulnerability as any threat actor on there can screenshot it and use it later for malicious intents. Cicada 3301 targets info such as healthcare, construction, finance and technology. This range of people this has impacted is a lot less than the puzzle as its recent and only reached info from UK, U.S, Canada and Japan.

10

Legacy

Although the last puzzle by Cicada may never be solved, Cicada 3301’s legacy is one that will be talked about for the rest of cybersecurity and internet history. What started off as a simple 4chan message turned into one of the greatest rabbit holes that people have ever encountered. Cicada 3301 changed the way people viewed cryptography and security. The dark web, which is often known for its illegal activities, turned into a place where people got together to solve these challenges. Cicada 3301 showed the world how TOR could be rightfully used, not just for its horrors. Liber Primus is not just a book that can’t be translated; it’s the remnants of history. Whether or not anyone will solve Liber Primus is something only time can tell. For all that we know, it may have already been solved, or it may never be solved. The book is so sophisticated that not even the world’s smartest and most powerful computer could solve its hidden message. Whether or not AI could be used is something that may be looked upon in the near future. Although Liber Primus would not be able to be decrypted even with a quantum computer based on the nature of its encryption, it’s very much possible that AI could be leveraged into scraping the dark web for any hints or clues to solve this problem. However, the question remains the same: What are you searching for? There is no direction, no hints, and no communication. Even if Liber Primus was somehow able to be decrypted, would it even be worth solving? No one knows what the book contains, and it might be all gibberish. All that we do know is that although Cicada 3301 may never appear again, their legacy will continue to question even the most intelligent and skilled cryptographers, security experts, and hackers.
