Evidence Acquisition Memorandum

A proposed process for securing, documenting, imaging, validating, and preserving a laptop and USB device for Case Study 1.

01

Executive Summary

This forensic investigation outlines our proposed plan for conducting a proper examination of Case Study 1. The purpose of this investigation is to secure, identify, and analyze evidence related to Employee X’s laptop and USB device. Our goal is to acquire all relevant evidence for management. This document explains the guidelines and methods we will follow to ensure the investigation is handled correctly from start to finish.

02

Scene Security and Initial Steps

The goal of this section is to outline the initial steps required to proceed with a proper investigation. If proper steps are not taken during the initial stages, the entire investigation may be compromised. Ensuring these first steps are completed is critical.

For physical security, the office in which the laptop and artifacts are located must be properly secured. Appropriate access controls should remain in place, whether through a security camera, security guard, or an organizational control such as ID scanning. The laptop device and model name should be documented, as should the USB model.

The laptop and USB must be photographed in their current location before being touched. Both are forms of original evidence, so documenting them is critical to the investigation.

Both the device and artifact must be isolated. The laptop should not be connected to any network and should remain powered off in a secure location. Every transfer or interaction with the evidence must be documented. The chain-of-custody form should record the date, time, location, receiver, person authorizing the transfer, and purpose of the exchange. This creates a complete and reliable record of the evidence for future use. (Nelson, Phillips, & Steuart, 2019, Chapter 1)

03

Evidence and Documentation

The goal of this section is to record all evidence important to the investigation. All relevant details about each device must be documented, including the device name, model, manufacturer, serial number, storage location, and condition. For the USB drive, the manufacturer, model, and storage size must also be recorded.

A chain-of-custody form must track who has custody of the evidence, the evidence location and description, the investigator’s name, model and serial number, and vendor name, as discussed above.

04

Evidence Acquisition Methodology

In order to acquire the evidence, specific steps must be taken to ensure it is handled properly. The laptop’s internal drive and the USB drive must undergo forensic imaging, which captures the data on each device. This includes active files, deleted files, and unallocated space, creating an exact duplicate of the original media.

A hardware write-blocker must be used during acquisition. The write-blocker allows investigators to connect the laptop’s storage device to a workstation without writing data to the original drive, preventing changes to files and protecting the integrity of the investigation.

After creating the forensic images, each image must be validated using cryptographic hashing. Both the original drive and forensic image should be hashed using SHA-256, the selected validation algorithm for this acquisition. The values can then be compared to confirm that the acquired image is identical and has not been altered. The images should be created using FTK Imager or a similar program, and the person performing the hashing and imaging should be recorded. (Kent et al., 2006)

05

Evidence Handling

This investigation will contain different types of evidence. They should be separated based on what they are and how they will be used.

Original Evidence
The laptop and USB. Both should be placed in approved secure containers.
Forensic Evidence
The forensic images taken from the laptop and USB. These should be fully secured and kept separate from the original evidence.
Working Copies
Copies of the forensic evidence used to preserve the original forensic images and maintain availability during the investigation.

(Nelson, Phillips, & Steuart, 2019, Chapter 1)

06

Risk Mitigation

Although investigators will work diligently to ensure that the investigation proceeds according to plan, points of failure may occur. The contents and condition of the laptop are unknown, and investigators cannot assume that everything works correctly.

Turning the laptop on may cause the system to overwrite information in memory or logs. If a write-blocker is not properly configured or used, files may be overwritten, which could be critical to the investigation. If a proper chain of custody is not maintained, the integrity of the evidence may also be compromised.

There is no way to remove every risk. This proposed plan accounts for these possibilities in order to mitigate as many of them as practical. (Nelson, Phillips, & Steuart, 2019, Chapter 1)
